## Terminology correction

The former `scorerConditionalRecall` name was misleading. Its implementation
counted candidate-retained true pairs at or above the review threshold, not merely
pairs that received features/scores and not pairs that survived alternative
retention. Evaluation version `matcher-evaluation-v0.2.0` now reports three
separate quantities:

- candidate-conditional feature-scoring coverage: `866/866 = 100%`;
- candidate-conditional review-threshold coverage: `866/866 = 100%`;
- candidate-conditional retained-link coverage: `864/866 = 99.7690531%`.

The former `falseUnmatchedRows` name is now `truthUncoveredARows`, because seven
of the 14 affected rows were routed to review but had no true pair among the
retained alternatives.

## Two post-score retention losses

| True pair | Generated | Score | Rank | Decision state | Retention cutoff | Loss reason |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| A000491 / B000713 | yes | 0.287754 | 4 | needs review | 0.25 | Top-three alternative limit |
| A000685 / B000767 | yes | 0.325287 | 4 | needs review | 0.25 | Top-three alternative limit |

Both scores exceed the 0.25 review threshold and retention cutoff. Neither loss
was caused by review threshold, margin, collision, contradiction, or score-window
logic. Each true pair ranked fourth and was intentionally excluded by
`maxAlternatives = 3`. Their top scores were 0.344076 and 0.402874, with small
top margins 0.033566 and 0.034122, so both A rows correctly remained in review.
This is the documented bounded-review tradeoff; the audit found no accidental
ranking or retention bug and did not change the policy.
